Design Thinking

Design Thinking is a human-centered approach to solving problems creatively. It helps designers understand users, generate innovative ideas, and create meaningful digital experiences.

💡

Divergent Thinking

Generate as many ideas as possible without judging them. Creativity comes before evaluation.

Example

Imagine designing a food delivery app. Think of 20 different homepage layouts before choosing one.

🎯

Convergent Thinking

Evaluate all the ideas and choose the one that best meets user needs and business goals.

Example

Select the layout that is easiest to use and fastest to navigate.

🧠

Lateral Thinking

Solve problems using unconventional and innovative ideas instead of traditional methods.

Example

Replace passwords with fingerprint or face recognition.

🌍

Context Thinking

Understand where, when and how users interact with your product.

Example

A village user may require offline access and larger buttons.

Know Your Audience

Before designing any product, designers must understand who will use it. Different users have different needs, goals, and challenges. Understanding your audience helps create products that are useful, accessible, and easy to use.

🎓

Student

Goal: Learn quickly and access study materials easily.

Needs: Simple navigation, dark mode, search feature.

Pain Point: Too much information on one screen.

👨‍🏫

Teacher

Goal: Manage classes and upload resources.

Needs: Attendance system and assignment upload.

Pain Point: Time-consuming manual work.

👵

Senior Citizen

Goal: Use digital services comfortably.

Needs: Large text, voice support, clear buttons.

Pain Point: Small text and confusing layouts.

User Personas

A User Persona is a fictional representation of a target user based on research. It helps designers understand user needs, goals, behaviors, and frustrations.

👩‍🎓

Priya

Age: 20

Occupation: Engineering Student

Goal: Learn quickly using simple educational apps.

Pain Point: Too much information on one screen.

Needs: Search option, dark mode, easy navigation.

👨‍💼

Rahul

Age: 35

Occupation: Project Manager

Goal: Track tasks efficiently.

Pain Point: Complex dashboards.

Needs: Clear reports and quick navigation.

👵

Lakshmi

Age: 65

Occupation: Retired Teacher

Goal: Use healthcare apps comfortably.

Pain Point: Small buttons and confusing layouts.

Needs: Voice guidance, larger text, simple interface.

Research Methods

User research is the process of understanding users' needs, behaviors, motivations, and challenges before designing a product. Good research leads to better user experiences.

👀

Direct Observation

Observe users while they perform real tasks. This helps identify usability issues that users may not mention.

Example

Watching a customer use an online shopping website to identify navigation problems.

📋

Surveys

Surveys collect opinions from a large number of users using questionnaires.

Example

"How satisfied are you with this mobile application?"

🎤

Interviews

Interview users individually to understand their expectations, frustrations, and goals.

Example

Asking students what difficulties they face while using online learning platforms.

🎯

Context & Goals

Understand where users interact with the product and what they want to achieve.

Example

Designing an offline-friendly learning app for students in rural areas.

Cognition & Behaviour

Understanding human cognition and behaviour helps designers build interfaces that are simple, intuitive, and enjoyable to use.

🧠

Cognition

Cognition refers to how people think, remember, learn and process information.

Example

A shopping app should use familiar icons like 🛒 so users immediately understand their purpose.

👆

Behaviour

Behaviour explains how users interact with buttons, menus, forms and navigation.

Example

Users expect the logo to return them to the Home page.

🔍

Self Exploration

Users enjoy discovering features on their own without needing instructions.

Example

Hover effects and tooltips help users explore naturally.

🏆

Gratification

Reward users after completing tasks to improve engagement.

Example

Showing "Congratulations!" after finishing a quiz motivates users to continue learning.

Information Architecture

Information Architecture (IA) is the process of organizing, structuring, and labeling content so users can easily navigate and find information. A well-designed IA improves usability, accessibility, and the overall user experience.

🗂️

Organization

Arrange information into logical groups so users can quickly understand the website structure.

🧭

Navigation

Provide clear menus, links, and navigation paths that help users move easily between pages.

🏷️

Labeling

Use meaningful names for menus, buttons, and categories so users instantly know where to go.

🔍

Search

Search functionality helps users locate information quickly without browsing every page.
